# What treating an uncertain relation as exact costs

Generated with Python 3.13.12, numpy 2.5.3 on Linux-6.18.44-fc-v24-x86_64-with-glibc2.39; source sha256 c16391f7b645, git 5eedb43dff. Latency columns are wall-clock on this machine and are not a claim.

A calibration measurement against a null that is true by construction. No real record appears; nothing here describes an instrument.

The kernel's consistency statistic assumes the relation `A x = b` is exact and only `b` carries error. That holds for a conservation law written on declared states. It fails for every constitutive relation the roadmap needs next — a routing row carrying K and x, a duty row carrying an effectiveness, a stage-capacity slope read off a survey — because those coefficients are measured quantities. `b_var` declared uncertainty on `b`; nothing declared it on `A`.

Each row below is 20,000 draws of a system where **the declared model is true**: `b` is generated by the relation that actually holds, the state estimate is drawn from its declared covariance, and nothing is faulty. A calibrated statistic has mean rank(A) = 2 and exceeds its 1% threshold about 1% of the time.

| declaration | mean statistic | rejection rate at the 1% threshold | times nominal |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A treated as exact | 7.077 | 25.92% | **25.9x** |
| A_var, rows declared independent | 2.001 | 1.21% | **1.2x** |
| A_var, full vec(A) covariance | 2.001 | 1.21% | **1.2x** |

### rows dependent, shared covariance 0.02

| declaration | mean statistic | rejection rate at the 1% threshold | times nominal |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A treated as exact | 8.280 | 29.56% | **29.6x** |
| A_var, rows declared independent | 2.140 | 2.11% | **2.1x** |
| A_var, full vec(A) covariance | 2.002 | 1.19% | **1.2x** |

**Treating an uncertain relation as exact is not conservative.** It rejects a system behaving exactly as declared up to 30 times as often as the threshold says, because the omitted term belongs in the denominator. An operator would be investigating instruments that are fine.

**And declaring the rows independent when they are not is a smaller version of the same error.** The roadmap says an independently declared `A_var` would not account for correlations from shared evidence. At the widest shared covariance swept, the per-row declaration rejects at 2.1x nominal against the full declaration's 1.2x. That is the cost of the shortcut, and it is much smaller than the cost of ignoring `A` altogether — which is the argument for the shortcut where evidence genuinely is not shared, not for using it everywhere.

**The correction is first order, and its leftover error behaves like one.** A correctly declared `A_var` above still rejects at 1.24x nominal rather than exactly 1. Scoring the same declaration while the coefficients get better known says whether that is the dropped second-order term or something else:

| declared coefficient variance | mean statistic | rejection rate | times nominal |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.02000 | 2.008 | 1.24% | 1.24x |
| 0.00500 | 1.989 | 1.00% | 1.00x |
| 0.00125 | 1.987 | 1.00% | 1.00x |
| 0.00020 | 1.992 | 0.98% | 0.98x |

It shrinks from 1.24x to 0.98x as the declared variance falls 100-fold, which is what a dropped second-order term does. The correction is not exact and this is the size of not being exact.

## What this does not fix

- The null is true by construction here, which is what makes this a calibration measurement and not a fault benchmark.
- Cov(E x) is first order in the coefficient error: the dropped term is the uncertain coefficients acting on the state error, second order in the two small quantities together. Its residual cost is measured above rather than assumed small.
- It is a quadratic form in the state, so a set declaring A_var has a different residual covariance at a different operating point and detectability stops being a property of the set alone.
- The projection is unchanged. A declared A_var corrects the consistency TEST; reconciliation still solves against A_bar as though it were exact, so a reported correction remains conditional on that.
- Nothing here declares dependence between A's error and b's, or between A's error and the state estimate. Shared evidence produces exactly those, and this experiment does not measure them.
- One relation shape, one operating point, one Gaussian coefficient error. A different relation could be more or less sensitive.
